The set of upper triangular matrices is a subgroup of GL2(R)

August 6, 2026

Proposition

Let

\[ G = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix} : a,b,c\in\mathbb R,\ ac\ne0 \right\}. \]

Then G is a subgroup of GL₂(ℝ).

Proof

Since \(\det\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix}=ac\ne0\) for every element of \(G\), each such matrix is invertible. We verify the three subgroup axioms.

(1) Closure under multiplication

Let \(A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix}\) and \(B=\begin{pmatrix}d&e\\0&f\end{pmatrix}\) be elements of \(G\). Then

\[ AB = \begin{pmatrix}ad&ae+bf\\0&cf\end{pmatrix}. \]

Since \(a,c,d,f\ne0\), \(ad\) and \(cf\) are nonzero. Hence \(AB\in G\).

(2) Identity element

The identity matrix \(I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) satisfies \(1\cdot1\ne0\), so \(I\in G\).

(3) Closure under inverses

For \(A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix}\in G\),

\[ A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac1a&-\frac{b}{ac}\\0&\frac1c\end{pmatrix}. \]

Since \(1/a\) and \(1/c\) are nonzero, \(A^{-1}\in G\).

Therefore G is a subgroup of GL₂(ℝ). □
