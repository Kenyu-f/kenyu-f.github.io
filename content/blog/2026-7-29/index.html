Legendre's theorem

July 29, 2026

Theorem (Legendre)

Let p be a prime number and let n ∈ ℕ. Then

\[ v_p(n!)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^k}\right\rfloor. \]

where vₚ(m) denotes the exponent of p in the prime factorization of the positive integer m.

Proof

For every positive integer m, let vₚ(m) denote the unique non-negative integer satisfying pvₚ(m) | m and pvₚ(m)+1 ∤ m.

Since n! = ∏i=1n i, the uniqueness of prime factorization implies

\[ v_p(n!)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_p(i). \tag{1} \]

For each positive integer i, observe that vₚ(i) = ∑k=1∞ 1pᵏ | i, where the indicator is 1 if pᵏ divides i and 0 otherwise.

Substituting this expression into (1), and exchanging the order of the finite nonzero sums, gives

\[ v_p(n!)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf 1_{p^k\mid i}. \]

The inner sum counts the positive integers at most n divisible by pᵏ. There are exactly ⌊n/pᵏ⌋ such integers. Therefore

\[ v_p(n!)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^k}\right\rfloor. \]

Only finitely many terms are nonzero, which completes the proof. □
