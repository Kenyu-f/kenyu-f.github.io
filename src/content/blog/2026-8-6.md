## Proposition
 
Let
 
$$
G=\left\{
    \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & c \end{pmatrix}
    \;\middle|\;
    a,b,c\in\mathbb{R},\ ac\neq 0
  \right\}.
$$
 
Then $G$ is a subgroup of $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$.
 
## Proof
 
Since
 
$$
\det\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & c \end{pmatrix} = ac \neq 0
$$
 
for every element of $G$, each such matrix is invertible; hence $G \subseteq GL_2(\mathbb{R})$. We verify the three subgroup axioms.
 
**(1) Closure under multiplication.**
 
Let
 
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & c \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix} d & e \\ 0 & f \end{pmatrix} \in G.
$$
 
Then
 
$$
AB=\begin{pmatrix} ad & ae+bf \\ 0 & cf \end{pmatrix}.
$$
 
Since $a,c,d,f\neq 0$, we have $ad\neq 0$ and $cf\neq 0$; hence $AB\in G$.
 
**(2) Identity element.**
 
The identity matrix
 
$$
I=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
 
satisfies $1\cdot 1\neq 0$, so $I\in G$.
 
**(3) Closure under inverses.**
 
For $A=\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & c \end{pmatrix}\in G$,
 
$$
A^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}
  \dfrac{1}{a} & -\dfrac{b}{ac} \\[2mm]
  0 & \dfrac{1}{c}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
 
Since $\dfrac1a\neq 0$ and $\dfrac1c\neq 0$, it follows that $A^{-1}\in G$.
 
Therefore $G$ is a subgroup of $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$. $\blacksquare$
