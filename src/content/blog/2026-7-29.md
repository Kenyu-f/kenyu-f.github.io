## Theorem (Legendre)

Let $p$ be a prime number and let $n\in\mathbb{N}$.
Then

$$
v_p(n!)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\left\lfloor
\frac{n}{p^k}
\right\rfloor,
$$

where $v_p(m)$ denotes the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorization of the positive integer $m$.

---

## Proof

For every positive integer $m$, let $v_p(m)$ denote the unique non-negative integer satisfying

$$
p^{v_p(m)}\mid m,
\qquad
p^{v_p(m)+1}\nmid m.
$$

Since

$$
n!
=
\prod_{i=1}^{n} i,
$$

the uniqueness of prime factorization implies

$$
v_p(n!)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_p(i).
\tag{1}
$$

Therefore, it suffices to compute the right-hand side.

---

For each positive integer $i$, observe that

$$
v_p(i)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\mathbf{1}_{p^k\mid i},
\tag{2}
$$

where

$$
\mathbf{1}_{p^k\mid i}
=
\begin{cases}
1,&\text{if }p^k\mid i,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Indeed, if $v_p(i)=e$, then

$$
p,p^2,\ldots,p^e
$$

divide $i$, whereas

$$
p^{e+1},p^{e+2},\ldots
$$

do not divide $i$.
Hence exactly the first $e$ terms of the series (2) are equal to $1$, and every remaining term is $0$.
Therefore,

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\mathbf{1}_{p^k\mid i}
=
e
=
v_p(i).
$$

Substituting (2) into (1) yields

$$
v_p(n!)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\mathbf{1}_{p^k\mid i}.
\tag{3}
$$

Since

$$
\mathbf{1}_{p^k\mid i}=0
\qquad
\text{whenever }
p^k>n,
$$

every summand in (3) is eventually zero.
Consequently, the double sum contains only finitely many non-zero terms, so the order of summation may be exchanged:

$$
v_p(n!)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\mathbf{1}_{p^k\mid i}.
\tag{4}
$$

Fix $k\ge1$.

The inner sum

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\mathbf{1}_{p^k\mid i}
$$

counts precisely the integers

$$
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
$$

that are divisible by $p^k$.

Such integers are exactly

$$
p^k,
2p^k,
3p^k,
\ldots,
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^k}\right\rfloor p^k,
$$

and there are

$$
\left\lfloor
\frac{n}{p^k}
\right\rfloor
$$

of them.
Hence

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\mathbf{1}_{p^k\mid i}
=
\left\lfloor
\frac{n}{p^k}
\right\rfloor.
\tag{5}
$$

Substituting (5) into (4), we obtain

$$
v_p(n!)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\left\lfloor
\frac{n}{p^k}
\right\rfloor.
$$

Finally, if

$$
k>\log_p n,
$$

then $p^k>n$, and therefore

$$
\left\lfloor
\frac{n}{p^k}
\right\rfloor
=
0.
$$

Thus only finitely many terms of the series are non-zero, so the above expression is well defined.

This completes the proof.

$$
\square
$$
